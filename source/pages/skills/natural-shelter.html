How to build a shelter from natural materials

Building a shelter from what the woods give you teaches you how shelter really works. It's also one of the best things to do with a group.

The short answerPick a safe spot with no dead branches overhead and out of any hollow. Prop a strong ridge pole on a tree fork, lean sticks against it, weave twigs across them, then pile on dead leaves until the layer is about an arm's length thick. Use only fallen wood, ask the landowner first, and take it down when you leave.

Lean-to or debris shelter?

A lean-to has a ridge pole between two supports and a roof on one side only. It's quick to build and open at the front, which is good with a fire in front of it, where a fire is allowed. A debris shelter has ribs on both sides and one closed end, like a long tent of leaves. It takes longer but it's much warmer.

Stay safe: never build under dead or hung-up branches, don't climb on a shelter, carry poles upright rather than across your body, and wear gloves for splinters.
