How to take a bearing with a compass

A bearing keeps you heading the right way when you can't see where you're going: in woods, in mist, or in the dark.

The short answerPut the edge of the compass along your route with the travel arrow pointing where you want to go. Turn the dial until its lines match the map's north-south grid lines, with the arrow pointing to the top of the map. Then hold it flat and turn yourself until the red needle sits in the arrow, and walk where the travel arrow points.

Tips

  • Walk to features on the bearing, one after another. It's far more accurate than staring at the needle while you walk.
  • In thick mist, send a friend ahead and steer them onto the line, then walk to them.
  • Know how far you're going. Measure it on the map, then count paces or check your time. Naismith's rule: allow an hour for every 5 km, plus a minute for every 10 m of climb.
  • Aim for something you can't miss, like a wall or a stream, then follow it to your target.
