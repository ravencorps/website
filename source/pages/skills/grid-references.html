How to read a six-figure grid reference

A grid reference tells anyone, including a mountain rescue team, exactly where you are. Six figures gets you to within 100 metres.

The short answerFind the grid square, read the number of the line on its left (the easting) along the bottom of the map, then the number of the line along its bottom (the northing) up the side. Split the square into tenths to add a third digit to each, and write the easting first: for example, SJ 515 774.

Tips

  • "Along the corridor, then up the stairs": eastings first, northings second.
  • Most compasses have a romer, a small scale on the baseplate, for measuring tenths accurately. Make sure it matches your map's scale (1:25,000 or 1:50,000).
  • Practise on places you know: your house, a car park, a café. Then check yourself with a phone app.
  • In an emergency, give the grid reference with its two letters, and a description of what's around you.
