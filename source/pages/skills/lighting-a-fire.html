How to light a fire safely and legally

A fire is one of the best things about a day outdoors, and one of the easiest to get wrong. Most of getting it right is knowing where you can't.

The short answerIn England and Wales, you need the landowner's permission to light a fire on their land. Your right to roam on open access land doesn't include lighting fires, and the Countryside Code says not to light fires and to have barbecues only where signs say you can. Where you're allowed, use a fire pit or a fire bowl, keep it small, never leave it, and drown it until the ashes are cold. For a hot drink, a camping stove is the safer choice.

Where you can and can't

  • Open access land: the right to roam under the Countryside and Rights of Way Act 2000 doesn't let you light or tend a fire, or do anything likely to start one.
  • Forestry England's forests: disposable barbecues are banned everywhere, and barbecues are only allowed in set places, such as fixed barbecues you can hire at some forests. Ask before lighting any fire.
  • Anyone else's land: only with the owner's permission. Damage caused by fire is charged as arson under the Criminal Damage Act 1971.
  • Where you can: your own garden (mind the neighbours and the smoke), campsites and bushcraft centres with fire pits, and land where the owner has agreed.
  • Scotland: different rules apply. See the question below.
At Raven Corps meetups we only light a fire where the landowner has agreed. At Delamere Forest, that means no fire unless Forestry England says yes.
