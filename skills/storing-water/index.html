How to store water for an emergency

You can go weeks without food but only days without water, so in any emergency plan, water comes first.

The short answerKeep at least 3 litres of drinking water per person per day for three days, which is the UK government's Be Ready advice, and more for cooking and washing. Sealed bottled water is easiest. If you bottle your own, use clean food-grade containers, label them with the date, keep them cool and dark, and refresh them regularly.

Step by step

  1. Work out how much you need

    The government's Be Ready checklist says about 3 litres per person per day for drinking, for three days. For a household of four that's 36 litres. Cooking and washing need more, so store extra if you can, and don't forget pets.

  2. Choose your containers

    The simplest store is sealed bottled water from the shop. To fill your own, use clean, food-grade containers with screw caps, such as water carriers sold for camping. Don't reuse milk bottles: they're hard to clean properly.

  3. Fill them from the tap

    Wash the containers in hot soapy water, rinse well, and fill them to the top with cold tap water. Screw the caps on tight.

  4. Label and store

    Write the date on each one. Keep them somewhere cool and dark, off a concrete floor if you can, and away from chemicals, fuel and cleaning products.

  5. Keep it fresh

    Shop-bought water keeps until its date while it stays sealed. Water you've bottled yourself should be used and replaced regularly; every six months is a common rule of thumb.

Questions

Does bottled water go off?

Sealed bottled water keeps until the date on the bottle. Once opened, treat it like tap water and use it within a day or two.

How do I make water safe to drink if the taps stop?

Boiling is the surest way without chemicals: let cloudy water settle, pour it through a clean cloth, then bring it to a rolling boil for one minute and let it cool with a lid on. Boiling kills germs but doesn't remove chemicals or fuel, so never drink floodwater.

Should I fill the bath if a water cut is coming?

It can help: a full bath gives you water for flushing the toilet and washing. Don't drink it, though, unless you've boiled it.

What does the water company do in a long outage?

If the supply is off for a long time, water companies usually hand out bottled water and tell customers where and when. Follow their updates and local news, and check on neighbours who can't easily get out.
