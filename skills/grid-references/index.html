How to read a six-figure grid reference

A grid reference tells anyone, including a mountain rescue team, exactly where you are. Six figures gets you to within 100 metres.

The short answerFind the grid square, read the number of the line on its left (the easting) along the bottom of the map, then the number of the line along its bottom (the northing) up the side. Split the square into tenths to add a third digit to each, and write the easting first: for example, SJ 515 774.

Step by step

  1. Find the grid square

    Ordnance Survey maps are covered in blue grid lines 1 km apart. Each line has a two-digit number printed in the map margin. Find the square your point is in.

  2. Read the easting

    Take the line on the left-hand (west) side of the square, and read its number along the top or bottom edge of the map. Say it's 51.

  3. Count tenths across

    Imagine the square split into ten strips from left to right. Count how many tenths across your point is. If it's halfway, that's 5, so your easting is 515.

  4. Read the northing

    Take the line along the bottom (south) side of the square, and read its number up the side of the map. Say it's 77.

  5. Count tenths up

    Now count how many tenths up the square your point is. If it's just under halfway, that's 4, so your northing is 774.

  6. Put it together

    Write the easting first, then the northing: 515 774. Add the two letters of the 100 km square, printed on the map, to make it unique in Britain: for example SJ 515 774.

Tips

  • "Along the corridor, then up the stairs": eastings first, northings second.
  • Most compasses have a romer, a small scale on the baseplate, for measuring tenths accurately. Make sure it matches your map's scale (1:25,000 or 1:50,000).
  • Practise on places you know: your house, a car park, a café. Then check yourself with a phone app.
  • In an emergency, give the grid reference with its two letters, and a description of what's around you.

Questions

What's the difference between a four-figure and a six-figure grid reference?

A four-figure reference, such as 5177, names a whole 1 km square. A six-figure reference, such as 515 774, narrows it to a 100 m square inside it, which is precise enough to find a path junction or a building.

Why do you read eastings first?

It's the convention, so everyone reads a reference the same way. Remember it as "along the corridor, then up the stairs": across first, then up.

Do I need the two letters at the start?

The numbers repeat every 100 km, so on its own a reference could be in several places in Britain. The two letters, such as SJ for most of Cheshire, make it unique. Always include them when you give your location to anyone else.

Can my phone give me a grid reference?

Yes. Ordnance Survey's apps can show your grid reference using GPS. It's still worth knowing how to read one from a paper map, because batteries run out and signal drops.
